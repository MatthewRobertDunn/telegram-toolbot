# Therapeutic Frameworks: Safe Self-Help-Level Use

These are brief educational summaries, not treatment protocols. Match the approach to the person's goals and preferences. Do not imply that a few chatbot exercises constitute a course of therapy.

## CBT-informed tools

CBT explores links between situations, thoughts, emotions, body responses and behaviour.

### Thought review
1. Describe one specific situation.
2. Identify the automatic thought.
3. Name the emotion and its intensity.
4. Separate observable evidence from assumptions.
5. Generate a balanced alternative, not forced positivity.
6. Decide whether any action is useful.

A balanced thought can acknowledge uncertainty: "I don't know what they meant. There are several possible explanations, and I can ask if it matters."

### Behavioural activation
When low mood leads to withdrawal, help the person choose a small, realistic activity linked to pleasure, mastery, connection or routine. Start small and review the effect. Do not frame low energy as laziness.

### Behavioural experiments
For ordinary, low-risk beliefs, identify a prediction and a safe way to observe what happens. Do not use experiments that expose the person to danger, abuse, medical risk or overwhelming distress.

## DBT-informed skills

DBT is a comprehensive treatment with a specific structure. The following are standalone skills inspired by DBT, not full DBT.

- **Mindfulness:** Notice what is happening now without immediately judging or acting.
- **Distress tolerance:** Use short-term grounding or sensory regulation to get through a difficult moment without making it worse.
- **Emotion regulation:** Identify the emotion, prompting event, vulnerability factors, action urge and a response that fits the facts.
- **Interpersonal effectiveness:** Clarify the objective, the relationship priority and self-respect priority before making a request or setting a boundary.

Do not claim to deliver DBT treatment or substitute a skills group, individual therapist or crisis plan.

## ACT-informed skills

ACT focuses on psychological flexibility and values-guided action.

- **Defusion:** "I am having the thought that..." can create a little distance from a thought without arguing with it.
- **Acceptance:** Make room for ordinary uncomfortable feelings when doing so serves a chosen value. Do not use acceptance language to encourage tolerating abuse or preventable harm.
- **Present-moment attention:** Notice sensations, sounds and surroundings.
- **Values:** Identify qualities the person wants to express, such as care, honesty or curiosity.
- **Committed action:** Choose one small action consistent with a value, even if discomfort is present.

## Motivational interviewing-informed change

Explore ambivalence without pressure. Ask what the person likes and dislikes about the current pattern, what they might want instead, and how ready they feel. Reflect their own reasons rather than supplying reasons for them.

## Interpersonal problem-solving

Help the person define the issue, identify what they can influence, generate options, consider likely consequences, and choose a proportionate next step. For unsafe or coercive relationships, prioritise safety and specialist support rather than communication exercises.

## Mindfulness and grounding

Offer brief, optional exercises. Some people find inward attention or breath focus uncomfortable, especially during panic, dissociation or trauma symptoms. Offer external grounding instead, such as naming objects in the room, feeling feet on the floor, or describing the immediate environment.

## Not for self-guided use

Do not lead:
- EMDR or bilateral-stimulation trauma processing.
- Prolonged exposure or trauma-memory exposure.
- Intensive trauma narrative work.
- Interoceptive exposure for panic without suitable clinical assessment.
- Any exercise likely to trigger severe dissociation, flashbacks or destabilisation.
