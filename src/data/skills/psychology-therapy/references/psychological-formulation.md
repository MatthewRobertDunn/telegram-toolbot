# Psychological Formulation and Analysis

## Purpose

A formulation is a provisional map of factors that may contribute to a difficulty and keep it going. It is not a diagnosis, a statement of certainty, or a complete account of a person.

## Biopsychosocial and contextual lens

Consider interacting influences rather than searching for one hidden cause.

### Biological and physical
Sleep, pain, illness, medication effects, hormones, energy, neurodevelopmental differences, sensory load and substance use may matter. Do not infer a medical cause or advise medication changes.

### Psychological
Attention, beliefs, expectations, memories, learning history, emotion regulation, coping habits, self-concept, motivation and uncertainty tolerance.

### Social and environmental
Relationships, family roles, work, housing, money, discrimination, culture, community, social support, safety and access to services.

## Pattern map

For a specific recurring event, map:

Situation or trigger
→ interpretation or prediction
→ emotion and body response
→ urge
→ action or avoidance
→ short-term consequence
→ longer-term consequence

This is a descriptive aid, not proof of causation.

## Maintaining cycles

Look for patterns such as:
- Avoidance reduces distress in the short term but may preserve fear or uncertainty.
- Reassurance can briefly soothe anxiety while increasing the urge to seek reassurance again.
- Withdrawal may reduce immediate conflict but increase isolation or unresolved tension.
- Perfectionistic standards may prompt overwork, exhaustion and further self-criticism.

Check whether the cycle actually fits the person's experience. Do not force a model onto them.

## Personality and individual differences

Discuss traits as dimensions and context-dependent tendencies, not fixed labels. A person may behave differently across settings, relationships and periods of stress. Avoid diagnosing personality disorders or assigning typologies from conversational data.

## Attachment and relationships

Attachment concepts can be useful as broad ways to discuss expectations of closeness, trust, support and separation. They are not a shortcut for diagnosing a person's attachment style or explaining every relationship problem. Consider the other person's behaviour and the actual relationship context.

## Cognitive and social psychology

Possible lenses include:
- Attention and memory biases.
- Attribution and perspective-taking.
- Social norms, roles and group influence.
- Learning, reinforcement and habit formation.
- Decision-making under uncertainty.
- Emotion and physiological arousal.

Use these as possible explanatory tools, not as labels or proof.

## Formulation template

**Concern:** What is troubling the person now?

**Context:** What was happening around the time it began or worsened?

**Possible triggers:** What tends to set it off?

**Responses:** Thoughts, feelings, bodily sensations and actions.

**Maintaining factors:** What may keep the pattern going?

**Strengths and resources:** What helps, even a little?

**Alternative explanations:** What else could account for the pattern?

**Next test or step:** What small observation or action could clarify things?

Keep the formulation concise and revise it when new information appears.
