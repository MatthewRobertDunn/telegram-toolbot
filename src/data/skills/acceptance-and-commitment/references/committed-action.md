# Committed Action

## Purpose

Committed action means building patterns of behaviour that serve chosen values. It includes goal-setting, skill development, problem-solving, persistence, flexibility, and recommitment after setbacks.

Action does not require confidence or the absence of discomfort. It should still be realistic, safe, and chosen.

## From value to action

Use this sequence:
1. Name the life direction in the person's own words.
2. Identify a behaviour that would express it.
3. Make the behaviour specific and small.
4. Check whether it is feasible in the person's actual circumstances.
5. Identify likely barriers.
6. Decide how to respond if a barrier appears.
7. Review and adjust without judgement.

## Make actions workable

A useful action is:
- observable: clear enough to know what it means;
- small: manageable rather than heroic;
- chosen: connected to a value the person endorses;
- contextual: realistic given time, money, health, responsibilities, and safety;
- flexible: open to revision when circumstances change.

## If-then planning

A simple plan can help:
- "If I notice myself postponing the email, then I will open the draft and work on it for five minutes."
- "If anxiety rises before the conversation, then I will notice my feet on the floor and say the first sentence I prepared."

Plans are aids, not contracts that must be obeyed regardless of context.

## Setbacks

When a plan does not happen:
- describe what happened without moral judgement;
- identify the obstacle or missing resource;
- ask whether the action was too large, poorly timed, or not truly values-based;
- adjust the plan;
- consider whether rest, support, or a boundary is the more values-consistent move.

Do not equate consistency with worth. A flexible return is often more useful than rigid persistence.

## Behavioural experiments

A person can try a small action to learn, not to prove they can succeed. Define what they want to learn and what would count as useful information. Avoid turning the outcome into a judgement of identity.

## Distinguish committed action from overextension

ACT is not a demand to push through exhaustion, illness, danger, or unfair conditions. Sometimes a values-guided action is asking for help, resting, saying no, or changing the plan.
