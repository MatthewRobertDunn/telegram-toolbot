# Present-Moment Awareness

## Purpose

Present-moment awareness is flexible contact with what is happening now. It can include sights, sounds, bodily sensations, thoughts, emotions, other people, and the task at hand.

The aim is not to empty the mind, achieve calm, or hold attention perfectly. Attention naturally wanders. Noticing and gently returning is part of the practice.

## Everyday mindfulness

Formal meditation is optional. Present-moment contact can be practised while:
- noticing the feel of feet on the floor during a conversation;
- listening to the full sentence someone is saying;
- attending to one step of a routine task;
- noticing sounds while walking;
- observing a thought and then returning attention to the chosen activity.

## Brief orienting practice

1. Invite the person to look around and name a few neutral objects.
2. Notice one or two sounds.
3. Notice contact with the chair, floor, or another stable surface, if comfortable.
4. Ask what is happening in the room right now.
5. Let attention widen to include both the surroundings and the person's current concern.

This can support orientation, but it is not a guaranteed grounding treatment. If it increases distress, stop and choose another approach.

## Attention as a flexible skill

Attention can move:
- inward, to thoughts and sensations;
- outward, to the environment and other people;
- towards a task or chosen action;
- broadly, to the whole situation.

The useful question is not "Am I mindful enough?" but "Where would it be useful to place my attention now?"

## Avoid

- promising that mindfulness will calm the person;
- insisting they close their eyes;
- requiring stillness or controlled breathing;
- treating distraction as failure;
- using mindfulness to avoid taking necessary action.
