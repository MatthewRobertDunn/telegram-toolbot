# Psychological Flexibility

## Working definition

Psychological flexibility is the capacity to contact the present situation, notice internal experiences with openness, and persist in or change behaviour in the service of chosen values.

It is not constant calm, confidence, happiness, or control. A person can feel anxious and still act flexibly. They can also feel calm while avoiding something important.

## Flexibility and inflexibility

Inflexible patterns often involve:
- becoming entangled with thoughts as literal truths or commands;
- struggling to eliminate feelings before taking action;
- losing contact with the present through rumination or worry;
- defining the whole self by a thought, role, or past event;
- losing sight of chosen values;
- acting automatically or abandoning meaningful action when discomfort appears.

These are descriptions of processes, not diagnoses or character flaws.

## The choice point

A practical way to map behaviour is to identify:
- the situation or trigger;
- difficult thoughts, feelings, sensations, or memories;
- the urge that follows;
- an "away move" that reduces discomfort or moves away from a valued direction;
- a "towards move" that serves a chosen value.

Away and towards are contextual descriptions, not moral categories. Sometimes leaving, resting, setting a boundary, or seeking safety is a values-consistent towards move.

## Signs of growing flexibility

- The person notices a thought without automatically obeying it.
- They can make room for some discomfort while acting.
- They can redirect attention when useful.
- They can distinguish themselves from a passing mental event.
- They can name a value in their own words.
- They can take a small step, adapt, or begin again after a setback.

Do not turn these into performance metrics. They are possible signs, not a scorecard.

## Questions that can help

- "What matters in this situation, apart from getting rid of the discomfort?"
- "What does this response do for you in the short term?"
- "What does it cost, if anything, over time?"
- "Is there a small action you would choose if the feeling could come along?"
- "Would opening up, changing the situation, or getting support be most useful here?"

## Avoiding a new control agenda

People can turn ACT itself into another rule: "I must accept this", "I should be mindful", or "I need to defuse correctly." Notice this gently. ACT processes are options for increasing choice, not standards to meet.
