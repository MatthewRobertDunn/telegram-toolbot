# Common Stuck Patterns

These are possible patterns, not diagnoses. Use them as hypotheses and check whether they fit the person's context.

## 1. Waiting to feel ready

**Possible process:** experiential avoidance; rule-governed behaviour.

**Helpful direction:** explore what "ready" means, whether waiting is helping, and whether a small values-guided step can happen alongside uncertainty.

**Avoid:** shaming the person for procrastinating or insisting they push through.

## 2. Reassurance loops

**Possible process:** attempts to obtain certainty or reduce anxiety that provide brief relief but maintain repeated checking.

**Helpful direction:** understand what reassurance does in the short term; explore uncertainty, practical risk, and values-guided responses.

**Avoid:** withholding necessary factual information or making unsupported assurances.

## 3. Harsh self-criticism

**Possible process:** fusion with evaluative thoughts; learned rules; attempts to motivate or prevent mistakes.

**Helpful direction:** acknowledge the pain, explore the function of the critic, practise defusion, and consider what a values-consistent response would look like.

**Avoid:** arguing that the person is wonderful or telling them to ignore all feedback.

## 4. Perfectionism

**Possible process:** rigid rules, fear of evaluation, avoidance of uncertainty.

**Helpful direction:** distinguish quality standards from inflexible demands; consider a workable "good enough" action and what matters beyond perfect performance.

**Avoid:** treating care, craft, or high standards as inherently unhealthy.

## 5. Rumination

**Possible process:** repeated analysis that may promise certainty, control, or problem resolution.

**Helpful direction:** distinguish solvable problems from repetitive mental review. If solvable, plan a concrete step. If not, practise noticing the loop and redirecting attention.

**Avoid:** dismissing legitimate reflection or asking the person to suppress thoughts.

## 6. Emotional overwhelm

**Possible process:** high arousal, limited attentional flexibility, unmet needs, or an external stressor.

**Helpful direction:** listen, check immediate safety and practical needs, reduce complexity, orient to the present if welcome, and consider support.

**Avoid:** launching into intense acceptance or body-focused exercises.

## 7. Loss of direction

**Possible process:** disconnection from chosen values, overload, competing demands, or low mood.

**Helpful direction:** start with one life area and a small question about what matters. Consider whether rest or support is needed.

**Avoid:** demanding a grand life purpose or a full life audit.

## 8. All-or-nothing action

**Possible process:** rigid rules about success, fusion with standards, avoidance after setbacks.

**Helpful direction:** define a smaller repeatable action, allow variation, and plan a flexible return.

**Avoid:** turning consistency into a moral test.

## 9. Acceptance becomes resignation

**Possible process:** misunderstanding acceptance as tolerating external harm or abandoning change.

**Helpful direction:** separate internal experience from external circumstances. Explore boundaries, advocacy, support, and practical change.

**Avoid:** encouraging endurance of abuse or unsafe conditions.

## 10. ACT becomes another performance demand

**Possible process:** fusion with rules such as "I must accept" or "I should be mindful."

**Helpful direction:** notice the rule, loosen the demand, and return to choice. It is okay if an exercise is not useful.

**Avoid:** grading psychological flexibility or insisting on technique compliance.
