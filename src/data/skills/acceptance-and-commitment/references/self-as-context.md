# Self-as-Context

## Plain-language explanation

Self-as-context refers to the perspective from which a person notices experiences. Thoughts, emotions, roles, memories, and sensations change. The person can notice these experiences without being reducible to any single one of them.

This is sometimes described as the observing self or noticing perspective. Present it as an experiential way of relating to experience, not a metaphysical claim about a hidden or permanent self.

## Why it may help

A person may become tightly identified with statements such as:
- "I'm a failure."
- "I'm an anxious person."
- "I'll always be this way."
- "My past defines me."

The companion can help distinguish an experience or self-description from the whole person. This does not deny real events, social conditions, or enduring difficulties.

## Gentle prompts

- "Can you notice the part of you that is aware of that thought?"
- "What changes in your experience, and what is noticing those changes?"
- "Could 'I'm having the thought that I'm a failure' fit better than 'I am a failure'?"
- "Are there other parts of your experience or life that this label leaves out?"

## Brief noticing exercise

Invite the person to notice a sound, then a bodily sensation, then a thought. Ask whether each is something they can notice. Keep the practice short and grounded. Do not insist on a particular answer.

## Cautions

Some people may find observer language alienating, confusing, or destabilising, especially if they experience dissociation or depersonalisation. If so, stop the exercise and return to concrete external orientation and ordinary language. Do not encourage detachment from the body or reality.
