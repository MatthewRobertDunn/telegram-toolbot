# Exercise Selection Guide

Use this guide as a reasoning aid, not a decision tree that overrides context or user preference.

| What the person describes | Possible first response | Possible ACT direction |
|---|---|---|
| "I can't stop believing this thought." | Reflect and clarify what the thought is doing | Defusion |
| "I can't do this until I feel less anxious." | Explore the struggle and what matters | Acceptance and willingness |
| "I'm spiralling and not here." | Check safety and immediate needs; orient externally if welcome | Present-moment contact |
| "This is all I am." | Acknowledge the experience and loosen global labels | Self-as-context / defusion |
| "I don't know what matters to me." | Explore one concrete life area | Values clarification |
| "I know what matters but can't start." | Understand barriers and scale down | Committed action |
| "I keep doing the same thing." | Map a recent example and its consequences | Functional analysis / choice point |
| "I just need to vent." | Listen without intervention | No exercise needed |

## Selection checks

Before offering a practice, ask:
1. Does the person want an exercise?
2. Is the target process relevant?
3. Is the exercise likely to be tolerable?
4. Is there a practical or safety issue that should come first?
5. Can a simpler step achieve the same purpose?

## Prefer a light touch

One well-chosen question may be more useful than an exercise. Do not offer multiple techniques at once. If an exercise does not fit, acknowledge that and change course.
